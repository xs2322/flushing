Unknowns

No dataset counts who actually needs a restroom where. Each piece is estimated as below: use the tiers to screen places, then check them on foot.

What it needsHow it guessesWhat that misses
People on footThe city sorts streets into five classes. Each class is given the middle value of the city's pedestrian counts for it: 15,129, 7,435, 5,862, 779 and 390 a day (Community and Baseline streets have almost no count sites). Subway entries within 400 m, shared among nearby blocks, are used if larger.Bus riders, visitors and street workers. Quiet streets have almost no counts behind them.
Visits a day (contracts only)A flat 1% of people on foot, capped at 215 uses a day for one unit. Not used for tiers.The 1% is an assumption. The cap is where the city's cost reaches procurement's $3 a use at $235,000 a year; the busiest Throne unit in Long Beach, California averaged 119 a day and peaked at 231. Throne's New York counts should replace both.
Walk timeStraight-line distance × 1.45, at 64 metres a minute.Routed walks on OpenStreetMap streets average 1.45 × the straight line, but vary: the middle half run 1.36 to 1.62. Highways, rail lines and large blocks make some walks much longer.
Who has nowhere to goNobody within 5 minutes of a restroom, everybody beyond 10, and a straight line in between.Restrooms that are listed as open but locked, or that need a phone to enter.
Greater needCensus shares for the people who live in the area, applied to the people walking through it.People without a home address, beyond the area around the city's nine drop-in centres. Shelter addresses are not published. Business districts, where residents and passers-by differ.
Poverty and complaintsPoverty share and yearly public-urination complaints within 200 m, each tiered on its own.Places where people do not call 311.
ThresholdsDOT's street classes; DOT's subway rule (more than 50,000 riders a week, about 7,100 a day, makes a Regional street); round numbers for the resident and poverty shares.Cut-offs are a policy choice. The presets show how much they matter: designing for residents or for passers-by changes the list.
Equal tiersA tier 5 from poverty and a tier 5 from foot traffic rank the same.They are different kinds of need. The deciding factor is always shown so readers can see which applies.
Fixed rulesPartly served (5 to 10 minutes) lowers a tier by one; a drop-in centre within 800 m in a straight line (about 18 minutes by the tool's own walk rule, not 10) sets at least tier 4, whoever lives on the block; resident factors need 10 or more residents; a subway station counts within 150 m and complaints within 200 m.Each is a judgement call, written down so it can be tested and changed.
Site checkLand owner, sidewalk width, flood plain, nearness to a subway station and parkland, from city data.Sun for the solar panel, tree lines and setbacks, bus stops and other curb uses that cannot move, structures below grade, lighting. These need a site visit.
Public landOwner names and types in the city's tax-lot records, plus park and plaza outlines.Setbacks from hydrants, trees and entrances; bus stops and loading zones in the curb lane. Sidewalk width is measured from the city's planimetric data, not surveyed.
Other restroomsRestrooms in the city's Public Restrooms list (parks, libraries, transit, plazas, privately owned public spaces), the MTA's subway restrooms and the Throne units. Every one is on the List page.Restrooms in cafés, stores, hotels and malls, which many people use. Commercial districts may look needier than they are.
Parks' own recordsParks' restroom inventory (1 Oct 2026) is matched to the City's list (2 Nov 2026) by location, one to one within 50 m. The City's list decides what counts.38 matched buildings disagree on whether they are open; 23 Parks buildings are not on the City list at all. Closure dates often look bulk-entered, the inventory records very few all-gender rooms, and project phases may lag behind work on the ground.
Throne sitesPlaced at the named intersection, or at the centre of the named park or plaza.The exact spot inside a park, which has not been published, and whether each unit is open yet.
