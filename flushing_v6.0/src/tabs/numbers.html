Testing the walk

Nobody walks in a straight line. You follow the sidewalk, go around the block, cross where you can, and find the one gate into a park. The map allows for this with one number, the detour factor: the real walk is about 1.45 times the straight line. It is extra distance, not waiting time; the slow walking speed (64 metres a minute, about 2.4 mph) is what leaves room for crossings and slower walkers.

To check the number, the team routed 587 real walks over OpenStreetMap's streets and paths, from a block's centre to its nearest open restroom, in four very different districts. The table looks at walks of 300 to 800 metres, the range where a block is decided to be within 10 minutes or not.

"Typical" is the median: half the walks were longer, half shorter. "Only the street part" leaves out the first few steps from the middle of the block to its sidewalk. On a perfect grid the factor would be about 1.27; real walks are longer because of superblocks, highways, rail lines and parks with one way in.

Where one number is unfair: Staten Island's curving streets need about 1.59, more than the 1.45 the map uses everywhere, so the map makes walks there look about 9% shorter than they are. Staten Island already rates Poor; its true gap is a little larger. A detour factor for each district is a planned improvement.
